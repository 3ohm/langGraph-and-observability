# P10: Drain within the 60-second hook

## Problem

A rolling update or a scale-down sends SIGTERM, then SIGKILL when the grace period ends. A worker that ignores SIGTERM loses the step in progress, and its runs wait for their leases to expire before another pod resumes them.

## What the template does

- On SIGTERM, `Worker.stop` stops intake, recovery and the pruner first, so no new run starts.
- It asks every active run to stop after its current step (`RunControl.request_drain`): the graph ends with `GraphDrained` at a checkpoint that it can resume from.
- It waits at most `APP_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (45), then cancels the runs that are still going.
- It returns this pod's runs to pending and clears their leases, so another pod's recovery (P9) resumes them at once, not after the lease expires.
- Then it stops the health server, closes the pool and flushes the traces.
- 45 s sits below the Deployment's `terminationGracePeriodSeconds: 60` and Compose's `stop_grace_period: 60s`. A step that outlasts the grace period is killed and runs again from its start on another pod (P11). That is safe because every step only reads, except publishing, which is idempotent (P13).
